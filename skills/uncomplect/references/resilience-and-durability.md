# Resilience and durability: terms and practitioners

**Scope: evergreen.** These terms describe failure, recovery, ownership, and observable behavior. They do not prescribe a runtime, broker, database, or effect library. The cited practitioners use these terms or explain the underlying distinction; this is not a claim of coinage.

A worker that restarts and a system that recovers its obligations are different things. Ask what state survives, who owns recovery, and which repeated actions remain safe. Keep the answer at the boundary that owns the invariant.

Use one lens when one lens answers the problem. Do not turn this reference into a list every review must recite.

The generic labels recovery owner, restart domain, durable scheduled intent, clean replay target, and deep failure boundary are our glossary labels. The book and section identify the underlying principle, not a claim that the author coined that label.

## Practitioner references

- **Nygard: capacity under failure.** Use _Release It!_ for timeouts, breakers, bulkheads, bounded results, and steady state. Ask which failing dependency can consume shared capacity.
- **Gray and Tate: core, boundary, worker, lifecycle.** Use _Designing Elixir Systems with OTP_ to separate domain rules from process machinery. Ask what a worker restart actually reconstructs.
- **Logan, Merritt, Carlsson, and Jurić: failure ownership.** Use _Erlang and OTP in Action_ and _Elixir in Action_ for dependency-aware recovery. Keep the general principle separate from their runtime’s mechanisms.
- **Bellemare: replay and its commit boundary.** Use _Building Event-Driven Microservices_ for stable replay, historical schemas, and processing guarantees. Ask which effects lie outside the guarantee.
- **Reis and Housley: repeated delivery and recovery gaps.** Use _Fundamentals of Data Engineering_ for idempotency, source conformance, and backfill. Ask what repeated delivery or an outage leaves incomplete.
- **Ousterhout: remove caller burden.** Use _A Philosophy of Software Design_, Chapter 10, to define avoidable errors away and locate recovery once. Keep information the caller needs for correctness visible.
- **Wlaschin: failure as domain data.** Use _Domain Modeling Made Functional_ to distinguish expected business failure, infrastructure failure, and panic. Ask whether independent validations should collect errors.
- **Normand: reduce interaction before coordinating it.** Use _Grokking Simplicity_, Chapters 15–17, to reduce shared resources and concurrent timelines. Ask what can become a calculation.
- **Hebert: test histories, then shrink them.** Use _Property-Based Testing with PropEr, Erlang, and Elixir_ for model state, valid command sequences, postconditions, and useful counterexamples. Plant a fault before trusting a property.
- **Google SRE contributors: aggregate behavior.** Use _Site Reliability Engineering_, Chapters 22, 26, and 27, for retry load, deadlines, independent integrity checks, and overload. Use Ewaschuk for user-facing signals.
- **Ambler and Sadalage: shared schema transitions.** Use _Refactoring Databases_ for the overlap between schema versions and consumer rollout. Ask who still needs the old representation.
- **Winters, Manshreck, and Wright: changes that converge.** Use _Software Engineering at Google_ for hermetic tests, deprecation discovery, and backsliding prevention. Ask what prevents the old dependency from returning.
