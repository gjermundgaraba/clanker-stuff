# Content and attribution patterns

Use when prose inflates significance, markets ordinary facts, relies on vague authorities, or fills gaps with speculation. These are editing cues, not proof of AI authorship.

## Inflation and borrowed authority

- **Inflated significance and superficial analysis:** remove unsupported symbolism, broad trends, and trailing “highlighting” or “reflecting” clauses. Retain meaningful stated relationships.
- **Promotional language and notability:** replace superlatives with the facts already supplied. Preserve specific media sources, numbers, and attributed opinions; do not invent a better citation or concrete detail.
- **Vague attribution:** retain uncertainty and flag missing sources if relevant. “Experts believe” cannot become a named study unless that study was supplied or separately verified within the task.
- **Vague associations:** replace “associated with”, “connected to”, or “linked to” with the relationship the source establishes. If the source gives no role or relationship, preserve its ambiguity rather than inventing one.
- **Formulaic challenges or upbeat conclusions:** keep the actual problems, plans, and author's assessment; remove ceremonial framing. Do not replace a vague conclusion with an invented expansion plan.

## Leftovers from chat and drafting

- **Speculative gap filling:** distinguish what the source says from what it establishes. Never turn “appears to have” into a definite date or replace an unknown biography with plausible details.
- **Chatbot residue:** remove offers to continue, generic praise, knowledge-cutoff boilerplate, and tutorial announcements when they are pasted into a deliverable. Preserve real limitations and qualifications.
- **Repeated heading:** remove an opening sentence that only repeats its heading; preserve one that adds information. Ordinary salutations and sign-offs in letters or comments are not chatbot residue.
- **Diff-anchored prose:** ordinary documentation should describe current behavior; release notes and migration guides can describe changes. Do not invent the current mechanism when the input only describes history.

## Fact-preserving examples

Before: “Her views have been cited in The New York Times, BBC, Financial Times, and The Hindu. She maintains an active social media presence with over 500,000 followers.”

After: “The New York Times, BBC, Financial Times, and The Hindu have cited her views. She has over 500,000 social media followers.”

Before: “While specific details about the company's founding are not extensively documented in readily available sources, it appears to have been established sometime in the 1990s.”

After: “The available sources suggest the company was founded in the 1990s, but give few details.”

Before: “Despite its industrial prosperity, Korattur faces challenges typical of urban areas, including traffic congestion and water scarcity.”

After: “Korattur has a prosperous industrial sector, but also faces traffic congestion and water scarcity.”

A rewrite improves wording; it does not fact-check the source by implication.
